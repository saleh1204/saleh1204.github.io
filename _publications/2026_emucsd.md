With the explosive growth of data generated by modern AI and ML workloads, there is a growing push to move computation closer to where data resides (at or near the storage layer) to reduce data movement costs and improve end-to-end performance. However, commercial computational storage devices (CSDs) often provide limited compute capabilities for demanding applications, and designing custom CSD hardware is prohibitively expensive, especially at low production volumes. As a result, researchers and system designers increasingly rely on emulation to explore architectural tradeoffs, prototype new features, and validate functionality before committing to hardware. This paper introduces EmuCSD, a flexible and low-overhead emulation framework for computational storage devices. EmuCSD supports a broad range of applications, allows users to model diverse compute-unit configurations, and can emulate multiple CSDs operating concurrently—capabilities that are critical for evaluating realistic, large-scale data-processing pipelines. The framework enables rapid design iteration, reduces development cost, and provides a practical environment for testing CSD behavior under different computational, storage, and workload scenarios. The experimental results demonstrate both the performance efficiency and functional versatility of EmuCSD, highlighting its value as a general-purpose platform to advance research and development in computational storage systems.
